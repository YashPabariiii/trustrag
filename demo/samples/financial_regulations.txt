MERIDIAN CAPITAL PARTNERS
Model Risk Management Standard — MRM-2026-01
Effective 1 April 2026. Owner: Head of Model Risk Management.
Synthetic document. Meridian Capital Partners is a fictional firm.

1. PURPOSE

This standard sets out the compliance requirements that apply to quantitative
models used in valuation, risk measurement, capital calculation, client
suitability and regulatory reporting at Meridian Capital Partners. It gives
effect to the firm's obligations under SR 11-7 (Supervisory Guidance on Model
Risk Management), the EU Artificial Intelligence Act where models fall within
its scope, and the firm's own internal control framework.

A model, for the purposes of this standard, is any quantitative method that
applies statistical, economic, financial or mathematical theory to input data
in order to produce an output that informs a business decision. This definition
includes machine learning systems and includes vendor-supplied models.

2. COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS

2.1 Model inventory. Every model must be registered in the firm's model
inventory before first production use. The register records the owner, the
developer, the validator, the risk tier, the date of last validation and the
date of next scheduled validation. Use of an unregistered model in a regulated
process is a reportable control breach.

2.2 Independent validation. Every Tier 1 and Tier 2 model is validated by a
team independent of the team that built it, before deployment and at least
annually thereafter. Validation covers conceptual soundness, the quality and
representativeness of input data, outcome analysis against realised results,
and benchmarking against an alternative approach.

2.3 Documentation. Model documentation must be sufficient for a competent
third party to reproduce the model's output from its inputs. Documentation that
depends on the continued availability of its author is not sufficient.

2.4 Change control. Any change to a model's methodology, its calibration, or
its input data sources requires re-validation proportionate to the change. A
recalibration on existing methodology requires a targeted review; a change of
methodology requires full re-validation.

2.5 Ongoing monitoring. Model performance is monitored continuously against
pre-agreed thresholds. Where a model breaches its threshold, the owner must
notify Model Risk Management within two business days and either remediate or
seek a documented, time-limited exception.

2.6 Retention. Model documentation, validation reports and monitoring records
are retained for seven years from the date the model is retired. Records
supporting a regulatory submission are retained for ten years.

3. RISK TIERING

Tier 1 models are those whose output feeds regulatory capital, financial
statement valuation, or client-facing suitability decisions. Tier 1 models
require annual independent validation, quarterly outcome analysis, and
board-level reporting of any threshold breach.

Tier 2 models inform material internal decisions but do not directly produce a
regulatory or client-facing figure. Tier 2 models require annual validation and
semi-annual outcome analysis.

Tier 3 models are used for exploratory or advisory purposes with human
judgement applied to every output. Tier 3 models require registration,
documentation and a periodic review, but not independent validation.

4. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND LANGUAGE MODELS

Systems built on large language models are subject to this standard in full.
Two additional requirements apply.

First, output traceability. Where a language model produces text that informs a
decision, the sources on which that text was based must be recorded alongside
the output. An answer without a traceable source is treated as an unvalidated
model output and may not be relied upon.

Second, continuous evaluation. Language model systems must be evaluated on
every production query, not on a periodic sample. The minimum measures are
faithfulness to source material, relevance of retrieved material, relevance of
the answer to the question, and an explicit hallucination measure. Results are
retained with the query and are reviewable by Model Risk Management on demand.

Vendor-hosted models must be version-pinned. A provider-initiated version
change is a model change under section 2.4 and requires re-validation before
production traffic is moved to the new version.

5. KEY FINDINGS OF THE 2025 THEMATIC REVIEW

Model Risk Management conducted a thematic review of the firm's twenty-eight
registered machine learning models during the fourth quarter of 2025. The
principal findings were as follows.

Finding 1. Eleven of twenty-eight models had no documented performance
threshold, and therefore could not breach one. Monitoring existed but had no
defined failure condition.

Finding 2. Documentation quality was the single largest driver of validation
delay. The median validation took 34 days, of which 19 were spent obtaining
information that should have been in the documentation pack.

Finding 3. Six models were operating outside their approved scope, in every
case through gradual expansion of use rather than a deliberate decision. None
had been re-tiered.

Finding 4. Language-model systems were the least well controlled category.
Three of four such systems recorded no per-query evaluation at all, relying
instead on periodic manual review of a small sample.

Finding 5. Where evaluation did exist, it was not attributed to a configuration
version, so changes in measured quality could not be traced to changes in the
system.

6. RECOMMENDED ACTIONS

The following actions were agreed with business owners and are tracked to
completion by Model Risk Management.

  1. Every registered model to have a documented performance threshold and a
     defined breach condition by the end of the second quarter of 2026.
  2. Documentation packs to be brought to the reproducibility standard in
     section 2.3, prioritised by risk tier.
  3. Annual scope attestation by every model owner, confirming that actual use
     matches registered use.
  4. Per-query evaluation to be implemented for all language-model systems,
     with results retained alongside the query.
  5. Evaluation results to record the retrieval and generation configuration
     that produced them, so that quality changes are attributable.
  6. A regression suite of validated question-and-answer pairs to be maintained
     for each language-model system and re-run after every configuration change.

7. BREACHES AND ESCALATION

A control breach under this standard is reported to Model Risk Management
immediately and to the Model Risk Committee at its next scheduled meeting.
Repeated breaches by the same owner are escalated to the relevant business
head and are reflected in the annual control assessment.

8. REVIEW

This standard is reviewed annually and out of cycle following any material
regulatory change or Severity 1 model incident.

Approved: Model Risk Committee, 6 March 2026.
