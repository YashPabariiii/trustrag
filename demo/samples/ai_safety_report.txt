NORTHWIND TECHNOLOGIES
Internal AI Safety and Deployment Policy — Version 3.2
Effective 1 March 2026. Owner: Office of the Chief AI Officer.
Synthetic document. Northwind Technologies is a fictional company.

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This policy governs the design, evaluation, deployment and monitoring of every
machine learning system Northwind Technologies operates, including systems
supplied by third-party vendors and systems built on external foundation
models. It applies to all business units in all jurisdictions in which the firm
operates. It does not apply to purely deterministic rule engines that contain
no learned component.

The policy exists because the failure modes of a learned system are different
in kind from those of conventional software. A conventional defect is
reproducible; a model failure is often statistical, silent, and only visible in
aggregate. The controls below are therefore weighted toward measurement rather
than inspection.

2. RISK CLASSIFICATION

Every system is assigned one of four risk tiers at design time, and the tier is
reviewed whenever the system's purpose or population changes.

Tier 1 (Prohibited). Systems that infer protected characteristics for the
purpose of differential treatment, or that operate autonomously on customer
funds without a human decision point. These are not deployed.

Tier 2 (High risk). Systems whose output materially affects a person's access
to credit, employment, housing or healthcare, and any system whose output is
presented to a customer as authoritative. Tier 2 systems require a documented
evaluation suite, a named accountable owner, quarterly bias testing, and a
rollback plan that can be executed within one hour.

Tier 3 (Limited risk). Internal decision-support systems where a human reviews
every output before it is acted upon. These require an evaluation suite and an
annual review.

Tier 4 (Minimal risk). Systems with no external effect, such as internal search
ranking or document classification for archival. These require an owner and a
monitoring dashboard.

Retrieval-augmented generation systems that answer questions from internal
documents are classified Tier 3 by default, and Tier 2 when their output is
shown to customers without human review.

3. EVALUATION REQUIREMENTS

No Tier 2 or Tier 3 system may be deployed without a written evaluation plan
approved by the Office of the Chief AI Officer. The plan must state what the
system is expected to get right, how that is measured, and what score
constitutes a failure.

For generative and retrieval-augmented systems specifically, the following four
measures are mandatory and must be recorded for every production query, not
merely for a held-out test set:

  (a) Faithfulness — the proportion of claims in the generated output that are
      supported by the retrieved source material. The minimum acceptable
      rolling average is 0.80.

  (b) Context relevance — whether the retrieval step surfaced material capable
      of answering the question at all. The minimum acceptable rolling average
      is 0.70. A low score here indicates a retrieval defect, not a model
      defect, and must not be addressed by prompt changes.

  (c) Answer relevance — whether the output addresses the question that was
      actually asked. The minimum acceptable rolling average is 0.75.

  (d) Hallucination rate — the proportion of outputs containing at least one
      unsupported claim. This must not exceed 0.10 for any Tier 2 system.

Where a measure falls below its threshold for three consecutive days, the
system owner must file a remediation plan within five business days. Where
faithfulness falls below 0.60 at any point, the system is withdrawn from
customer-facing use until remediated.

4. HUMAN OVERSIGHT

Tier 2 systems require a reviewer who is competent to overturn the system's
output and who is measured on the quality of their overrides, not on their
throughput. A reviewer who approves more than 98% of outputs over a quarter is
presumed not to be exercising meaningful oversight, and the arrangement is
escalated to the Office of the Chief AI Officer.

Oversight is not a checkbox on a screen. A reviewer must have access to the
source material the system used, the confidence or evaluation scores attached
to the output, and a documented route to reject it without penalty.

5. MONITORING AND INCIDENT RESPONSE

Every deployed system emits, at minimum: request volume, latency at the 50th
and 95th percentiles, its four evaluation measures, and the rate at which
outputs are overridden by a human reviewer. These are reviewed weekly by the
system owner and monthly by the AI Risk Committee.

An AI incident is any of the following: a measure breaching its threshold for
more than 24 hours; an output that causes or would have caused financial or
reputational harm; the discovery that a system has been operating outside its
approved scope; or a third-party model change that alters behaviour without
notice.

AI incidents are reported through the standard operational incident process
within four hours of detection. The AI Risk Committee reviews every Severity 1
and Severity 2 AI incident within ten business days.

6. THIRD-PARTY MODELS

Systems built on externally hosted foundation models carry an additional
control: the provider's model version must be pinned, and any provider-side
version change is treated as a new deployment requiring re-evaluation against
the existing evaluation suite before traffic is moved.

Northwind does not accept a vendor's own evaluation results as evidence. Every
vendor system is evaluated against Northwind's own reference dataset.

7. DATA GOVERNANCE

Training and retrieval corpora must have a documented lineage: where each
document came from, who owns it, what licence governs its use, and when it was
last reviewed. Documents whose licence does not permit machine processing are
excluded at ingestion, not at query time.

Personal data used in training is subject to the firm's existing retention
schedule. A model trained on personal data that has since been deleted must be
retired or retrained at the next scheduled review.

8. RECOMMENDED ACTIONS FOR SYSTEM OWNERS

The Office of the Chief AI Officer recommends the following, in order of
expected benefit:

  1. Instrument evaluation before instrumenting anything else. A system without
     per-query evaluation cannot be said to be monitored.
  2. Maintain a golden set of at least twenty question-and-answer pairs per
     system and run it after every configuration change.
  3. Treat a fall in context relevance as a retrieval problem and a fall in
     faithfulness as a generation problem. Conflating the two produces months
     of prompt engineering against a retrieval defect.
  4. Record the retrieval configuration alongside every evaluation result, so
     that a change in score can be attributed to a change in configuration.
  5. Review the reviewer. Oversight that always agrees is not oversight.

9. REVIEW

This policy is reviewed annually by the AI Risk Committee, and out of cycle
whenever a Severity 1 AI incident occurs or applicable regulation changes.

Approved: AI Risk Committee, 12 February 2026.
